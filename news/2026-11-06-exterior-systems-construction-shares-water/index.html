Exterior Systems Construction Shares Water Intrusion Testing Resources in Los Angeles

A new guide, YouTube video and podcast episode explain water intrusion testing for Los Angeles property owners and managers.

By Exterior Systems ConstructionUpdated 2026-11-06Los Angeles, California

  • The guide is available online, the video is on YouTube and the audio is on SoundCloud.
  • Contact the team to discuss the affected area, previous repairs and testing scope.

LOS ANGELES, California (November 6, 2026) Exterior Systems Construction has released a guide, a YouTube video and a podcast episode about water intrusion testing in Los Angeles. An audio version is also available on SoundCloud.

The guide gives local property owners and managers an introduction to assessing moisture concerns before deciding what repairs may be needed. It explains the role of testing in identifying where water enters a building, helping readers understand why locating the source matters when planning a response.

The material is relevant to homeowners, homeowners associations, property managers and commercial clients dealing with leaks or moisture in single-family homes, multi-unit buildings and managed properties. It also provides context for related planning tools, including building envelope testing, moisture mapping and scheduled inspections.

For someone dealing with a recurring leak or considering work after an earlier repair, the resources offer a starting point for discussing the affected area, what has already been tried and what evaluation or testing may be appropriate. Identifying where moisture is entering can help guide repair planning for exterior systems such as roofs, balconies, stairs, drainage and stucco. The guide does not replace an assessment of a specific building, but it can help readers prepare for that conversation.

Readers can find the guide online, watch the video on YouTube or listen to the audio version on SoundCloud. The podcast episode covers the same topic. Exterior Systems Construction invites people with moisture concerns to contact the team to discuss the location and history of the problem and the right evaluation scope.
